# Writing for 12–13-year-olds

All student-facing revision notes, questions, hints, example answers, feedback,
glossary entries and score summaries are written for students aged 12–13.
Apply this to every topic, difficulty and question style, including printable tests.

- Ask a direct question about one clear learning point. Use short sentences.
- Use everyday words for instructions: “name”, “what”, “how” and “why”.
- Keep science terms needed for school, such as alveoli, amplitude and enzyme.
  Explain unfamiliar terms in plain words in the notes or feedback.
- Increase challenge through reasoning and applying knowledge, not harder wording.
- Recall questions ask for a fact without showing the answer in the prompt.
- Explain questions ask the student to describe an idea or give a reason.
- Application questions use a short, familiar situation.
- Spot-the-mistake questions give a clear incorrect statement and ask for a correction.
- Missing-word questions accept the missing word, not the whole sentence.
- Use short, complete explanations that teach the idea. Do not replace help with
  “this is the glossary definition” or instructions intended for teachers.
- Keep answer choices at the same reading level and keep the correct answer in the choices.
- Keep IDs, topic filters, source references and progress keys stable when editing wording.
- Check scientific accuracy. Simplifying language must not change the meaning.

Read every new or changed question together with its answer and explanation.
Check that they agree. Run `npm test` and the existing verification scripts.
The automated wording checks are guardrails; they do not replace editorial review.
